# Neuroscience-to-IDA evidence map

Review snapshot: 8 September 2026. This is a structured, broad scoping map, not a completed systematic review of all neuroscience. It covers 28 data resources, 13 measurement families, 14 methodological/inference problems and 10 research routes. The source register has 55 primary or official references, including methods, trials, repositories, software and engineering documentation. Those categories must not be counted as 55 independent efficacy studies.

## Search and inclusion approach

Targeted web searches and direct official-source checks covered: EEG/MEG/MRI/iEEG repositories; sleep, stress/affect, treatment-response and longitudinal clinical data; cellular/connectomic/molecular archives; signal artifacts, circular analysis and generalization; randomized feedback/intervention evidence; and acquisition/timing standards. Searches included author/title phrases, modality plus dataset/repository, and manufacturer protocol terms. Primary studies and official repository/manufacturer documentation were preferred. Secondary summaries were used only to locate primary records.

The map includes resources when they represent a distinct measurement scale, experimental design or plausible IDA test. It does not claim every resource was downloaded. Only the two previously reported IDA datasets have completed numerical analyses here. Other records are catalogue/method reviews. No common participant identifiers or pooled correlations are invented across datasets. Ranking is an analyst judgment about next-experiment utility, not an estimated probability of solving mental illness.

Access failures are retained: DEAP's page failed; iEEG exposed a JavaScript shell; GEO presented a browser challenge; the NSD download portal was unavailable but its primary paper was found. ABCD's current official documentation points approved access to NBDC, so an old unrestricted NDA workflow would be incorrect. NEMAR/OpenNeuro overlap is explicitly flagged. Raw licenses, consent, asset sizes, versions and suitable outcome variables remain study-level gates.

## Evidence units and inference

Repository metadata establishes that a resource exists and describes its intended scope. It does not establish a new finding. A cross-validated predictor does not establish a causal target. A randomized treatment contrast can support the assigned package in its studied population, without identifying a physiological mechanism or optimal policy. Invasive and animal experiments need a separate bridge to a wearable human intervention. The atlas makes those transitions visible in each record.

The failure register separates demonstrated problems, known inference limitations, and IDA hypotheses. It does not assert that all neuroscientists make these mistakes. Many of the repairs originate within neuroscience itself. IDA's contribution must therefore be assessed against those existing methods, rather than claiming novelty for artifact control, state-space modelling, dimensional psychiatry or adaptive trials alone.

## Completeness ledger

The measurement-family map is broadly populated. Dataset-level access/reuse audits, full-text risk-of-bias reviews, trial-registry screening and meta-analysis are not complete. There is no defensible numerical denominator for “all neuroscience” in this review. Deep coverage of pain, social cognition, speech/language, autonomic disorders, developmental differences, medication interactions and population diversity remains open. The headband design is also a baseline awaiting physical and clinical validation.

For each future addition, record: DOI/URL and snapshot; species/population; modalities and raw availability; units/reference/montage; timing and treatment assignment; repeated sessions; target availability; participant/site overlap; access/license; missingness; fit/evaluation leakage risks; specific IDA test; and result/stop condition. A study without an independent later outcome may validate acquisition or a representation, but cannot by itself validate durable stabilization.

The best next mechanistic test is whether a feasible pre-decision measurement changes which input is beneficial and whether that gain persists beyond input delivery. Existing evidence makes that worth testing. It does not guarantee an accessible perfect intervention for every state.
